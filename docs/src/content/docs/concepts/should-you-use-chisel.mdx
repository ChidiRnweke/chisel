---
title: Should you use chisel?
description: Chisel is highly opinionated and not configurable. When to adopt it as-is, and when to fork it and encode your own rules.
---

import { Card, CardGrid } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

Chisel enforces one specific way of building software: FastAPI backends,
SvelteKit frontends, layered services, statelessness, shadcn components,
one assert per test. The rules are not configurable. Beyond per-line and
per-file suppression with a reason, you cannot turn a rule off or tune it.
That rigidity is what makes the signal deterministic, and it means chisel
is a great fit for some teams and the wrong fit for others.

  <Card title="Adopt it as-is" icon="approve-check">
    Your stack is FastAPI and/or SvelteKit, and you don't yet hold strong
    architectural opinions. Chisel gives you a coherent, battle-tested set
    of them on day one, plus the loop that makes your agent respect them.

    You already have opinions, or your stack is different (Django, React,

    but the machinery transfers. Be inspired, fork, and encode your own
    rulebook.

## If you have no strong opinions yet

Take chisel's. They are internally consistent, they optimise for the thing
that matters in agentic engineering (code that stays readable and
evolvable), and the checker never argues about them. You can always
develop your own taste later and fork then; starting with no enforced
architecture at all is the worst of the options.

## If your opinions differ, or your stack does

Fork it. The value of chisel is not the specific rules; it is the shape of
the tool: a deterministic checker, a skill per layer that teaches the
pattern, and a JSON contract that points every violation at the skill that
explains it. That shape works for any stack.

The codebase is built to be extended: a new rule is one check method in a
service plus one `describeRules()` entry, and the CLI discovers it
automatically. See [Architecture](/chisel/concepts/architecture/) for how
the pieces fit.

Whatever you do, don't skip the idea itself. Agentic engineering needs a
deterministic architecture signal; the only real choice is whether you
adopt this one or build your own.
